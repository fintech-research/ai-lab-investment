# AGENTS.md — paper/

Rules for working in the `paper/` directory. Global rules are in `@../AGENTS.md`.

## Writing Style

- **No bold paragraph headings in the main body.** In `_introduction.qmd`, `_calibration.qmd`, `_valuation.qmd`, `_discussion.qmd`, and `_conclusion.qmd`, `**Bold text.**` as a pseudo-heading is forbidden — use `###`/`####` for subdivisions, or just start the paragraph. Run-in bold labels *are* the established convention in `_model.qmd`'s preemption-equilibrium derivation and throughout `_appendix.qmd` (proof steps, verification blocks, robustness exercises); match the surrounding file there rather than converting them.
- Concise, formal prose. Top finance/management journal style (current target ladder starts at Management Science; see `../submission/README.md`). No filler.
- Equations: use Quarto cross-references `@eq-name`, figures `@fig-name`, tables `@tbl-name`.

## Paper Structure

Main entry: `index.qmd` (includes all sections, then references, then `_appendix-cover.qmd` + `_appendix.qmd`). Sections: `_introduction.qmd` (which itself includes `_literature.qmd`), `_model.qmd`, `_calibration.qmd`, `_valuation.qmd`, `_discussion.qmd`, `_conclusion.qmd`, `_appendix.qmd`. Bibliography: `references.bib`; style files `econometrica.bst`, `title.tex`, `keywords.tex`; render config `_quarto.yaml`.

## Blind submission build

`index-blind.qmd` is the double-anonymous copy used for Management Science (`just render-blind`; see `../submission/README.md`). It includes the *same* section files as `index.qmd` — **if you add or remove an include in one, do the same in the other** — but drops `authors`/`affiliations`/`thanks` and sets `linestretch: 1.5`. Its `title`, `abstract`, and `keywords` are duplicated from `index.qmd` (Quarto has no per-document metadata include); `tests/test_paper_metadata.py` fails if the two drift apart, if the keyword count leaves 3–5, or if `keywords.tex` stops matching. Profile selection lives in `_quarto.yaml` (`identified` = default, `blind`); `split_blind_pdf.py` cuts the rendered PDF into the manuscript and the e-companion at the `_appendix-cover.qmd` page.

`_appendix.qmd` is the **Internet Appendix** — always refer to it that way in prose ("Internet Appendix A", not "Appendix A"). Its sections: A. Proofs (Propositions 1–3 plus Numerical Finding 1, Dario's dilemma) and the result-taxonomy table; B. Numerical Verification Methods (including the block *The preemption game in the exact primitives* with `tbl-duopoly-beliefs`, `tbl-duopoly-order`, `tbl-spread-entry`, `tbl-duopoly-leverage`, followed by three blocks on the nested pure-power game); C. Calibration Details and Data Sources (incl. the archetype-by-archetype sourcing and the φ̂ evidence blocks); D. Parameter Sensitivity; E. Robustness (parameter sensitivity, Cournot discussion + quantified fixed-pie contest, dynamic φ reallocation, duopoly Dario's dilemma, alternative regime structure); F. Single-Firm Illustrations in Regime H (`fig-option-value`, `fig-comparative-statics`); G. A Normalized Scale-Gap Diagnostic (`fig-growth-decomposition`); H. Credit Spreads and Default Probabilities (`fig-credit-risk`). The cover page (`_appendix-cover.qmd`) carries an appendix-only table of contents built with `etoc`: the `\etocdepthtag.toc{ia}` after the `## Internet Appendix` heading separates appendix entries from main-text ones. Sections F–H, the derivations block at the top of B, `@tbl-firms-data` in C, and the discussion block at the end of E were demoted from the main text to hold it near the Management Science revision cap (32 pages at 1.5 spacing); keep the main-text pointers in sync if you edit them. The main text carries one table, `@tbl-domains` in `_model.qmd`'s conventions subsection, collecting the operating assumptions, result domains, and measured approximation sizes: add a row there rather than a new qualifying paragraph elsewhere.

Results are labeled by method: Propositions 1–3 for analytical results, **Numerical Finding 1** for Dario's dilemma. Do not promote a numerical finding to a proposition; `@tbl-result-taxonomy` in Internet Appendix A records the analytical status of each result and must stay in sync.

## Proof Verification

The closed-form algebra of Propositions 1–3 is machine-checked in Lean 4/Mathlib (`../lean/`), and Internet Appendix A opens with a paragraph describing exactly what is and is not formalized. If you change a closed form, a first-order condition, or a comparative static, check whether the corresponding Lean theorem and that scope paragraph need updating (`lean/README.md` maps theorems to paper results).

## Key Model Facts (for editing proofs/text)

- Baseline: r=0.12, μ_L=0.01, μ_H=0.06, σ=0.25, λ=0.10, α=0.40, γ=1.50, δ=0.03
- β_L⁺ ≈ 3.01, β_H ≈ 1.55 (positive characteristic roots)
- Model of record (single firm, issue #176): the exact piecewise problem, in which the firm may invest before or after the switch. Assumption 1 is (A1) and (A2) only. The former (A3), (1−1/β_L⁺)/α ≈ 1.67 ≥ 1, is now the descriptive *L-regime insufficiency condition* (`eq-l-insufficiency`) with no role in the solution; the former (A4) is a property of the functional form.
- Two versions of the exact problem, keep the labels distinct in prose: *precommitted post-switch scale* (K_H* after the switch; Proposition 1(iii), always a finite trigger) and *scale chosen at the switch* (unrestricted; at baseline the single firm waits, waiting margin 1.08, and a finite pre-switch trigger exists only for λ < 0.073, or γ ≥ 1.9).
- The pure-power form F_L ∝ X^{β_H} is an *approximation* (a decision rule, not a solution of the HJB), written X^pp, K^pp = K_H*. Error at baseline: trigger 49% and capacity 57% below the precommitted-scale policy; value loss 2.6% against that policy, 6.9% against waiting; φ* exact. The calibration and valuation sections still use it (re-solved in #179 to #183) and write X*, K* for X^pp, K^pp; `@sec-conventions` says so. The duopoly section does not: since #178 its X_P, K_L, φ_L, X_F*, K_F are the equilibrium of the exact game, and the reduced-form game of the earlier draft is the *nested pure-power game* of Internet Appendix B.
- φ̲ ≈ 0.18 (faith-based survival threshold, A_eff-channel), Ω ≈ 0.22; exact net threshold φ̃ ≈ 0.32 (both channels, eq-phi-tilde, λ-dependent); φ*(λ) > φ̃(λ) iff λ ≳ 0.034 at baseline
- Baseline results, single firm (model of record): φ* ≈ 0.70 (closed form); post-switch X_H* ≈ 0.0028, K_H* ≈ 0.0067 (closed form); pre-switch X* ≈ 0.0093, K* ≈ 0.0158 with precommitted post-switch scale; no pre-switch trigger with scale chosen at the switch. Pure-power approximation: X^pp ≈ 0.0047, K^pp ≈ 0.0067.
- Beliefs (`tbl-beliefs`): K* is not belief-invariant (hump-shaped in λ, peak near 0.19); the precommitted trigger rises in λ up to about 0.13 then falls; the unrestricted trigger rises in λ wherever it exists; only the pure-power trigger falls in λ (above λ ≈ 0.024).
- Baseline results, duopoly (model of record, issue #178: exact primitives, leader's (K_L, φ_L) chosen at the entry demand, follower's scale chosen at the switch, ℓ = 0 with the option to stop): rent-equalization trigger X_P ≈ 0.0086, K_L ≈ 0.0141, φ_L ≈ 0.688 against φ* ≈ 0.701; the follower waits for the switch at every demand level and enters at X_F* ≈ 0.086 with K_F ≈ 0.32, all in training (K_F/K_L ≈ 23, X_F*/X_P ≈ 10); leading at X_P is worth 1.22 times mutual waiting; leader's boundary 0.00146 (X_P/X_D ≈ 5.9). Without the option to stop X_P ≈ 0.0090; with precommitted post-switch scale X_P ≈ 0.0084. The numbers come from `src/ai_lab_investment/models/duopoly_equilibria.json` and are pinned in `tests/test_duopoly_exact.py`.
- What X_P is and is not: it is the lowest demand at which leading is as good as following (first up-crossing on a demand scan; existence numerical, uniqueness and subgame perfection not shown). It is NOT established as the date of first investment: the trigger X_L of a firm that knows it will lead is lower at every λ on the grid (X_L ≈ 0.0034 ≈ 0.40 X_P at baseline, follower better off by 10% there; nearly equal to X_P at λ = 0.50), so between X_L and X_P the game has a second-mover advantage (a war of attrition), not a race. Write "rent-equalization trigger", say that first investment lies between X_L and X_P, and do not write that preemption dissipates a first-mover rent at baseline.
- Do not write for the duopoly: "φ is role-invariant" (the factorization gives φ* as a critical point only against a rival at φ*; the leader is 1.9% below φ* at baseline, 19% at λ = 0.03, 0.2% at 0.50; the post-switch follower trains with everything), "capacity is invariant to beliefs" or "optimists invest earlier" (X_P and K_L are hump-shaped in λ with a maximum at 0.10 on the grid), a "preemption discount of 43%" (the single firm has no pre-switch trigger at baseline; against the precommitted-scale single firm X_P is 7% lower), or "the spread at entry is weakly increasing in λ" (it is U-shaped under both debt conventions).
- Leverage in the equilibrium (Remark 4, `tbl-duopoly-leverage`): under par debt at r_c < r, ℓ = 0.40 raises K_L by 68%, K_F by 69%, X_P and X_F* by 36%, the leader's boundary by 52%; under fairly priced debt it lowers each of the first four by less than 1% and still raises the boundary by 20% (X_P/X_D 5.9 → 4.9). Leverage effects on scale and timing are the subsidy; effects on the boundary survive.
- Follower hitting times (C24): from X_P the follower's trigger is reached before the switch with probability 0.7%, with probability 21% if the switch never came, and 80 years after the switch in expectation; expected entry about 98 years after the leader's. The firm exposed to default is the leader (7% chance of reaching its boundary before the switch at ℓ = 0).
- Nested pure-power game (earlier draft; Internet Appendix B, and what the calibration and valuation sections still use): X_F ≈ 0.12, K_F ≈ 0.26, X_P ≈ 0.0026, X_L^mono = X^pp ≈ 0.0047, discount 44.5% (87.1% with a re-optimized leader); against the exact game its X_P is 69% and K_L 52% too low.
- Credit primitives (issue #177): recovery = (1−b)·[inference business net of the operating cost of its capacity, δ(1−φ)K/r]⁺, capped at the unlevered asset value U(X_D) and at C_D/r (eq-recovery); the unlevered firm has a shutdown boundary (eq-default-boundary at C_D = 0), so X_D, equity and default probability are continuous at ℓ = 0; a fair-coupon solver (D = ℓI(K) at issuance) sits beside the par-debt-at-r_c convention. At the single-firm policies recovery is zero at every leverage. Precommitted-scale policy, par debt: X_D 0.00148 / 0.00160 / 0.00170 and spread 17 / 20 / 23 bps at ℓ = 0 / 0.40 / 0.70; fair debt: 0.00148 / 0.00178 / 0.00201 and 17 / 26 / 35 bps (coupon rate 12.3% at ℓ = 0.40). The gross-recovery rule of the earlier draft stays callable (`recovery_rule="gross"`).
- Credit risk figure (fig-credit-risk, fixed X=0.10, K=1, φ=0.5; evaluation point to be revisited in #179): spreads ≈28/46/79/147 bps at ℓ=0.05/0.20/0.40/0.70 (benchmark = r; 0/12/41/97 under the gross rule); 5-yr default (first-passage, L-regime drift) 0.63%/1.80%/4.85%/12.98%
- Internet Appendix E (fixed-pie contest, sweep, duopoly dilemma) still quotes the nested pure-power game, with pre-#177 values (0.57 / 0.63, 0.53 to 0.63), until #180 and #182.
- Full-model value of the uninvested firm in λ (fig-lambda-option-value, `lambda_option_value_curve`, exact by default, `pure_power=True` for the approximation): *decreasing* for λ ≲ 0.025 (where φ*(λ) < φ̲), then increasing, convex up to λ ≈ 0.05 (0.085 in the approximation) and CONCAVE beyond; over the policy range [0.1, 0.5] it is increasing and concave, so the news-asymmetry prediction is that bad timeline news moves valuations more than good news
