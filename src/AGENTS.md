## Model Architecture

**Hierarchy:** `models/parameters.py` → `base_model.py` → `duopoly.py` → `valuation.py`

**Two model modes** — do not confuse them:
- *Simple* (no φ): `installed_value()`, `optimal_trigger_and_capacity()` — for H-regime analysis using combined `A_L`/`A_H`.
- *Full* (with φ): `installed_value_with_phi()` matches paper eq-a-eff. The single-firm entry points return the **model of record**, the exact piecewise problem (issue #176): `solve()` gives a `SingleFirmSolution` (whether a finite pre-switch trigger exists, the precommitted-scale policy, the value of waiting, the post-switch policy, the pure-power error), `optimal_trigger_capacity_phi()` the precommitted-scale (X*, K*, φ*), `option_value_with_phi()` the exact value.
- *Pure-power approximation*: `pure_power_trigger_capacity_phi()`, `pure_power_option_value()`, and in the duopoly every `DuopolyModel.pure_power_*` method. The former full-model solution, kept so its error can be reported and the earlier draft's numbers reproduced. `valuation.py`, `robustness.py`, `calibration/` and most paper figures are still pinned to it (comments name the issue, #179 to #184, that re-solves each); do not switch one of them without re-pinning its tests and paper numbers.

**Duopoly (`duopoly.py`, issue #178):** two layers, do not mix them. *Model of record*: `DuopolyModel.solve(scale="envelope", debt="par"|"fair")` returns a `DuopolySolution`, the preemption equilibrium of the game in the exact primitives with a leader that chooses (K_L, φ_L) at the demand at which it enters, with the fixed-scale leader variants, the single-firm benchmark and the nested approximation alongside; `exact_game()` returns the underlying `LeveredPreemptionGame`. One solve takes one to two minutes (five to eight under fair debt, half an hour where the follower also enters before the switch; the two fair-debt cells at λ = 0.05 use the narrower scan of `SCAN_OVERRIDES`), so the equilibria the paper quotes are cached in `models/duopoly_equilibria.json` (`load_equilibrium_grid()`, `cached_equilibrium()`); regenerate with `just duopoly-grid` (about an hour on 8 workers) after any change to the game, and re-pin `tests/test_duopoly_exact.py`. *Nested approximation*: `pure_power_preemption_equilibrium()`, `pure_power_preemption_reoptimized_leader()`, `pure_power_follower()`, `pure_power_leader_monopolist()` and the methods built on them (`comparative_statics`, `leverage_comparative_statics`, `summary`, used by the exploratory pipeline) are the reduced-form game of the earlier draft: follower option and dilution priced with β_H and the unconditional A_eff, leader held at the pure-power scale. There is no `solve_preemption_equilibrium` any more, on purpose: a caller must say which game it wants.

**Credit primitives (`duopoly.py`, issue #177):** recovery is `recovery_value()`: (1 − b) times the inference business net of the operating cost of its capacity (`liquidation_value()`), floored at zero and capped at `unlevered_asset_value()` at X_D and at C_D/r; `recovery_rule="net"` (default), `"net_until_switch"` (cost capitalized at r + λ) or `"gross"` (the earlier draft's rule, kept so its credit numbers reproduce). There is no zero-leverage special case: at ℓ = 0 `default_boundary()` is the shutdown boundary (the coupon-zero case), `equity_value()` includes the shutdown option, and `default_probability()` is the probability of reaching that boundary; do not reintroduce early returns at ℓ ≤ 0. `default_boundary`, `equity_value`, `debt_value`, `firm_value`, `recovery_value` take an explicit `coupon=`; `fair_coupon(X, ...)` solves D = ℓ·I(K) at the issuance demand, and `ValuationAnalysis.credit_metrics` / `credit_table` / `credit_spread(debt_convention="par"|"fair")` report both conventions. These closed forms use the single-boundary reduction. The exact game (`preemption_levered.py`) has its own claims with the coupled regime-switching boundary and solves under par or fair debt; the nested `pure_power_*` solvers use par debt only.

**`symbolic_duopoly.py`** — verification/documentation module using SymPy. Not called in the pipeline. Used to verify that `base_model.py` and `duopoly.py` implement the correct ODE solution.

**`piecewise_option.py`**: core solver of the single-firm model of record, the *exact* piecewise L-regime stopping problem (the HJB forcing term is the H-regime option only below X_H*, and the exercised H payoff above it). First half: `PiecewiseOptionModel` (two-region closed form at fixed (K, φ), `precommitted_optimum()` for the demand-free optimal policy, pure switching, the exercise envelope, the Brennan--Schwartz LCP), `solve_single_firm()` / `SingleFirmSolution`, and the belief comparative statics (`belief_comparative_statics`, `belief_trigger_ranges`, grid `BELIEF_GRID`). Second half (from `reduced_form_reference` on): verification, the error of the pure-power approximation against the exact solution (`piecewise_bias`, `envelope_bias`, the sweeps and dilemma re-checks behind Internet Appendix B). `optimal_policy(X_0)` states the policy at an evaluation demand and returns the best *immediate* investment when X_0 is above the trigger (low λ); use `precommitted_optimum()` unless reproducing those reports. Run `uv run python -m ai_lab_investment.models.piecewise_option [sweep]` for the solution, belief table and error report.

**`capability_stock.py`**: research prototype for issue #174 (not in the pipeline). Its default specification is the unrestricted single-firm problem, and `solve_single_firm()` uses it for the unrestricted policy and the value of waiting; the added primitives feed no paper number. Solves the exact pre-switch stopping problem under four added primitives: a late-entry discount `theta`, a late-training effectiveness for entrants and for incumbents' redeployed compute (`late_training`, `redeploy`), a fixed installation cost, and an accumulating capability stock. The defaults are the paper's exact problem, so `CapabilityStockModel(params)` reproduces the unrestricted `PiecewiseOptionModel` numbers, and `pure_power_policy(params, beta=params.beta_H)` reproduces the reduced model. The solution is one scalar root (algebraic below the entrant's trigger, one quadrature above it), checked by the Brennan--Schwartz solver of `piecewise_option.py` through `model.lcp` and by a policy-iteration LCP on the exercise envelope (`joint_lcp`). Run `uv run python -m ai_lab_investment.models.capability_stock` for the tables behind the issue comment; the numbers are pinned in `tests/test_capability_stock.py`.

**`preemption_exact.py`**: the L-regime preemption game in the exact primitives for firms that operate forever and carry no debt (issue #173), the base class of the model of record: the leader's and follower's values solve the L-regime ODE with the follower's exact post-switch option as forcing (Green's function, `ForcedClaim`), under both post-switch scale assumptions of Internet Appendix B, with an independent finite-difference/LCP check (`finite_difference_subgame`). `ExactPreemptionGame.rent_equalization` holds the leader at the reduced-form (K*, φ*); `rent_equalization_reoptimized` lets the leader choose scale at entry. Run `uv run python -m ai_lab_investment.models.preemption_exact` for the λ × γ table (about ten minutes). Pinned in `tests/test_preemption_exact.py`. `pre_switch_entry` takes an optional exact payoff, which `preemption_levered` uses.

**`preemption_levered.py`**: the same game with the credit primitives of #177 (issue #178): every installed firm may stop operating (shutdown boundary at ℓ = 0, default boundary with debt), debt is priced with the recovery rule, and the coupon is the par coupon or the fair coupon at issuance. `LeveredPreemptionGame(ExactPreemptionGame)` overrides the subgame (`LeveredSubgame`: `leader_value`, `follower_value`, `leader_claims(coupon)`, `leader_credit(X)`); the rent-equalization and leader-optimization routines are inherited. Claims are built from `PiecewisePowers` forcings and `SegmentClaim` (closed-form Green's function, value and slope), plus a tabulated `ForcedClaim` when the follower's scale is chosen at the switch; `installed_claims()` is the exact (coupled) equity and debt of an installed firm. Conventions are listed in the module docstring (rival never exits, zero recovery after the switch, coupon fixed at issuance). `equilibrium_record()` is one solve as a flat dict; `hitting_statistics()` and `expected_entry_time()` are the first-passage objects; `python -m ai_lab_investment.models.preemption_levered` rewrites the cache.

**`robustness.py`** — the ±25% one-at-a-time parameter-perturbation sweep behind Internet Appendix E. Re-computes the dilemma asymmetry, capacity-gap fraction, faith thresholds, and preemption discount at each draw, and *reports* (A1)/(A2) failures instead of truncating them. Run `just run-sweep`, or via the `tasks.robustness_sweep` pipeline flag. Its paper-pinned numbers live in `tests/test_robustness.py::TestPaperNumbers` — update the appendix text and the test together.

**`calibration/`** — `data.py` holds the four stylized firm archetypes (`get_stylized_firms()`: Anthropic-, OpenAI-, Google-, xAI-like) and the baseline calibration; `revealed_beliefs.py` infers implied λ from observed investment. Revealed beliefs appear in the slides and the paper's conclusion (future work), not as a paper section.

## IMPORTANT: Figure Generation

`figures/paper.py` is the single source of truth for all paper figures (11 `create_*` functions). Never add model computations elsewhere.

The other `figures/` modules produce exploratory output for the Hydra pipeline (`phase1.py`, `phase2.py`, `phase4.py`, `phase5.py`) and write to `RESULTS_DIR`. They do **not** feed the paper — do not edit them when a paper figure needs to change.

## Testing

Tests are in `tests/` (one file per module; about 800 tests). `tests/test_duopoly_exact.py` covers the duopoly model of record: the claims against independent finite-difference solves, the cached equilibria against rent equalization and optimality of the leader's policy, and the paper's duopoly numbers; tests that re-solve an equilibrium are marked `slow` and run with `just test-slow` (the default `just test` deselects them). `tests/test_duopoly.py` tests the credit closed forms and the nested pure-power game. `tests/test_base_model.py` pins the single-firm model of record (`TestSingleFirmSolution`, `TestBeliefComparativeStatics`, `TestProposition1ClosedForm`) and keeps everything about the pure-power approximation in `TestPurePowerApproximation`. Run with `just test`. `assert` statements allowed in tests. Several tests pin paper numbers (e.g. `TestAppendixERobustness` in `test_valuation.py` checks the Internet Appendix E tables) — if a model change moves those numbers, update the paper text and the test together.

The closed-form algebra these modules implement is independently machine-checked in Lean (`../lean/`, see `lean/README.md`). Changing a closed form means checking the corresponding Lean theorem too.
