## Model Architecture

**Hierarchy:** `models/parameters.py` → `base_model.py` → `duopoly.py` → `valuation.py`

**Two model modes** — do not confuse them:
- *Simple* (no φ): `installed_value()`, `optimal_trigger_and_capacity()` — for H-regime analysis using combined `A_L`/`A_H`.
- *Full* (with φ): `installed_value_with_phi()` matches paper eq-a-eff. The single-firm entry points return the **model of record**, the exact piecewise problem (issue #176): `solve()` gives a `SingleFirmSolution` (whether a finite pre-switch trigger exists, the precommitted-scale policy, the value of waiting, the post-switch policy, the pure-power error), `optimal_trigger_capacity_phi()` the precommitted-scale (X*, K*, φ*), `option_value_with_phi()` the exact value.
- *Pure-power approximation*: `pure_power_trigger_capacity_phi()`, `pure_power_option_value()`, and in the duopoly every `DuopolyModel.pure_power_*` method. The former full-model solution, kept so its error can be reported and the earlier draft's numbers reproduced. The decompositions in `valuation.py`, `calibration/` and most paper figures are still pinned to it (comments name the issue, #183 or #184, that re-solves each; the credit exhibits are at the model of record since #179, Dario's dilemma since #180 and the robustness sweeps since #182); do not switch one of them without re-pinning its tests and paper numbers.

**Duopoly (`duopoly.py`, issues #178 and #200):** two layers, do not mix them. *Model of record*: `DuopolyModel.solve(scale="envelope", debt="par"|"fair")` returns a `DuopolySolution`, the equilibrium of the entry game in the exact primitives with a leader that chooses (K_L, φ_L) at the demand at which it enters, with the fixed-scale leader variants, the single-firm benchmark and the nested approximation alongside; `exact_game()` returns the underlying `LeveredPreemptionGame`. Two triggers, never to be confused: `X_L` is the benchmark date of first investment (one firm leads at the trigger it prefers given that its rival follows; `equilibrium_verified` says the no-deviation checks hold) and every record key without a prefix is evaluated there (`K_L`, `phi_L`, `X_F`, `K_F`, `spread`, `distance_to_default`, the hitting statistics); `X_P` is the rent-equalization trigger, an upper bound on that date, and the same objects there carry the prefix `rent_eq_`. One solve takes three to four minutes (ten to fifteen under fair debt, half an hour or more where the follower also enters before the switch; the two fair-debt cells at λ = 0.05 use the narrower scans of `SCAN_OVERRIDES` and `ENTRY_SCAN_OVERRIDES`), so the equilibria the paper quotes are cached in `models/duopoly_equilibria.json` (`load_equilibrium_grid()`, `cached_equilibrium()`); after any change to the game regenerate with `just duopoly-grid`, which keeps the cached X_P solves and recomputes the rest (about an hour on 8 workers), or `just duopoly-grid-fresh`, and re-pin `tests/test_duopoly_exact.py`. *Nested approximation*: `pure_power_preemption_equilibrium()`, `pure_power_preemption_reoptimized_leader()`, `pure_power_follower()`, `pure_power_leader_monopolist()` and the methods built on them (`comparative_statics`, `leverage_comparative_statics`, `summary`, used by the exploratory pipeline) are the reduced-form game of the earlier draft: follower option and dilution priced with β_H and the unconditional A_eff, leader held at the pure-power scale. There is no `solve_preemption_equilibrium` any more, on purpose: a caller must say which game it wants.

**Credit primitives (`duopoly.py`, issues #177 and #179):** recovery is `recovery_value()`: (1 − b) times the inference business net of the operating cost of its capacity (`liquidation_value()`), capped at `unlevered_asset_value()` at X_D and at C_D/r; `recovery_rule="net_with_shutdown"` (default since #179, `DEFAULT_RECOVERY_RULE`: the business valued with its owner's option to shut it down at `inference_shutdown_boundary()`, single-boundary form, cost at r), `"net"` (the #177 rule, no option, floored at zero), `"net_until_switch"` (cost capitalized at r + λ) or `"gross"` (the earlier draft's rule, kept so its credit numbers reproduce). The exact game takes `"net_with_shutdown"` (default) or `"net"` (`LeveredPreemptionGame(recovery_rule=...)`, `LeveredPreemptionGame.liquidation_value`); the cache is solved under the default. There is no zero-leverage special case: at ℓ = 0 `default_boundary()` is the shutdown boundary (the coupon-zero case), `equity_value()` includes the shutdown option, and `default_probability()` is the probability of reaching that boundary; do not reintroduce early returns at ℓ ≤ 0. `default_boundary`, `equity_value`, `debt_value`, `firm_value`, `recovery_value` take an explicit `coupon=`; `fair_coupon(X, ...)` solves D = ℓ·I(K) at the issuance demand, and `ValuationAnalysis.credit_metrics` / `credit_table` / `credit_spread(debt_convention="par"|"fair")` report both conventions. These closed forms use the single-boundary reduction. *On-policy credit (issue #179):* `ValuationAnalysis.on_policy_credit()` prices debt issued at the single firm's precommitted-scale policy (as an installed monopolist, `LeveredPreemptionGame.installed_credit`), at the leader's first investment X_L (policy of the cached unlevered equilibrium held fixed across leverage) and at the follower's post-switch entry, with the exact claims, over `ON_POLICY_LEVERAGES` and both debt conventions; `credit_phi_profile()` varies φ at those scales; `single_firm_closed_form` in `roles` gives the closed-form counterpart. These back `tbl-credit-spread`, `tbl-credit-pd` and `fig-credit-risk` (pinned in `tests/test_valuation.py::TestPublishedCreditLevels`); the off-policy point `CREDIT_RISK_DEMAND_LEVEL` is no longer in the paper. *Installed value in λ (issue #181):* `ValuationAnalysis.installed_value_vs_lambda()` holds (X, K, φ, coupon) fixed and returns the going-concern value with its closed-form λ-derivatives (Corollary 1) and the exact equity with the option to stop, for the installed monopolist (`installed_claims`) or the leader before follower entry (`subgame(...).leader_claims`); `equity_value_vs_lambda_with_phi` is the re-optimized option, not installed value. Pinned in `tests/test_valuation.py::TestValueInLambda`; the identities are `symbolic_duopoly.installed_value_in_lambda`. The exact game (`preemption_levered.py`) has its own claims with the coupled regime-switching boundary and solves under par or fair debt; the nested `pure_power_*` solvers use par debt only.

**Dario's dilemma (`belief_mismatch.py`, issue #180):** the cost of investing on a belief λ_invest when the truth is λ_true, with every value exact under the truth (no pure-power pricing). Losses are shares of the informed firm's value at a reference demand X_0 held fixed across beliefs (default half the informed trigger: X*/2 for the single firm, X_L/2 for the duopoly) and are *not* invariant to X_0; a firm whose own trigger is below X_0 invests at once at the scale its belief prescribes there. `SingleFirmMismatch(params, lambda_true, version)` has two versions, never to be merged: `"precommitted"` (Proposition 1(iii), the main-text Numerical Finding 1; every belief invests) and `"unrestricted"` (scale chosen at the switch; beliefs ≥ 0.073 wait and lose nothing at baseline). `.loss()` returns the loss and its timing / capacity / allocation components (timing = investing at the mistaken date with the truth's best policy for that date; the rest split by averaging the two orders), `.levered()` the same on the levered claims with the distance to the stop boundary and the five-year first-passage probability (policies stay the unlevered ones), `matched_pairs()` the additive, proportional and horizon pairs. `DuopolyMismatch` computes the mismatch inside the X_L equilibrium: a mistaken *leader* best-responds under its belief to a rival that follows and waits for the switch (for beliefs ≥ 0.075 this is the cached equilibrium policy at that belief); a mistaken *follower* loses nothing unless it enters before the switch or takes the lead, in which case roles change and the outcome is the mistaken leader's. One belief takes one to two minutes, so the paper's cells (13 beliefs × 2 roles × leverage {0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.15, 0.40} × fair/par) are cached in `models/duopoly_dilemma.json` (`load_dilemma_grid()`, `cached_dilemma_cell()`; regenerate with `just dilemma-grid` after any change to the game or to `duopoly_equilibria.json`). `ValuationAnalysis.dario_dilemma`, `dario_dilemma_matched`, `dario_dilemma_leveraged`, `dario_dilemma_surface`, `dilemma_third_derivative` and `dario_dilemma_duopoly` delegate to it; the earlier draft's versions are `pure_power_dilemma`, `pure_power_dilemma_leveraged`, `pure_power_dilemma_policy_value`, `pure_power_dilemma_third_derivative` and `pure_power_dilemma_duopoly` (the last is the non-equilibrium comparison of Internet Appendix E); nothing outside their own tests and that comparison calls them since #182.

**`symbolic_duopoly.py`** — verification/documentation module using SymPy. Not called in the pipeline. Each symbolic function returns the expressions it derives and residuals that simplify to zero when the stated identity holds (closed forms of Propositions 1 and 2, the piecewise L-regime solution, φ̃, the recovery split, the Tullock factorization, the follower's capacity condition, the Green's function); `notebooks/model_derivation.ipynb` displays them and `tests/test_symbolic_duopoly.py` asserts them. A zero residual verifies that identity only.

**`piecewise_option.py`**: core solver of the single-firm model of record, the *exact* piecewise L-regime stopping problem (the HJB forcing term is the H-regime option only below X_H*, and the exercised H payoff above it). First half: `PiecewiseOptionModel` (two-region closed form at fixed (K, φ), `precommitted_optimum()` for the demand-free optimal policy, pure switching, the exercise envelope, the Brennan--Schwartz LCP), `solve_single_firm()` / `SingleFirmSolution`, and the belief comparative statics (`belief_comparative_statics`, `belief_trigger_ranges`, grid `BELIEF_GRID`). Second half (from `reduced_form_reference` on): verification, the error of the pure-power approximation against the exact solution (`piecewise_bias`, `envelope_bias`, the sweeps and dilemma re-checks behind Internet Appendix B). `optimal_policy(X_0)` states the policy at an evaluation demand and returns the best *immediate* investment when X_0 is above the trigger (low λ); use `precommitted_optimum()` unless reproducing those reports. Run `uv run python -m ai_lab_investment.models.piecewise_option [sweep]` for the solution, belief table and error report.

**`capability_stock.py`**: research prototype for issue #174 (not in the pipeline). Its default specification is the unrestricted single-firm problem, and `solve_single_firm()` uses it for the unrestricted policy and the value of waiting; of the added primitives only the pre-positioned capability of `StockSpec.pre_positioned` feeds the paper, as the extension of Internet Appendix E (`robustness.capability_extension`, issue #182), never the model of record. A trigger below the smallest capacity searched (next to the upper bound of (A2)) is reported as `status == "immediate"` with X* = 0, not as an error. Solves the exact pre-switch stopping problem under four added primitives: a late-entry discount `theta`, a late-training effectiveness for entrants and for incumbents' redeployed compute (`late_training`, `redeploy`), a fixed installation cost, and an accumulating capability stock. The defaults are the paper's exact problem, so `CapabilityStockModel(params)` reproduces the unrestricted `PiecewiseOptionModel` numbers, and `pure_power_policy(params, beta=params.beta_H)` reproduces the reduced model. The solution is one scalar root (algebraic below the entrant's trigger, one quadrature above it), checked by the Brennan--Schwartz solver of `piecewise_option.py` through `model.lcp` and by a policy-iteration LCP on the exercise envelope (`joint_lcp`). Run `uv run python -m ai_lab_investment.models.capability_stock` for the tables behind the issue comment; the numbers are pinned in `tests/test_capability_stock.py`.

**`preemption_exact.py`**: the L-regime preemption game in the exact primitives for firms that operate forever and carry no debt (issue #173), the base class of the model of record: the leader's and follower's values solve the L-regime ODE with the follower's exact post-switch option as forcing (Green's function, `ForcedClaim`), under both post-switch scale assumptions of Internet Appendix B, with an independent finite-difference/LCP check (`finite_difference_subgame`). `ExactPreemptionGame.rent_equalization` holds the leader at the reduced-form (K*, φ*); `rent_equalization_reoptimized` lets the leader choose scale at entry. Run `uv run python -m ai_lab_investment.models.preemption_exact` for the λ × γ table (about ten minutes). Pinned in `tests/test_preemption_exact.py`. `pre_switch_entry` takes an optional exact payoff, which `preemption_levered` uses.

**`preemption_levered.py`**: the same game with the credit primitives of #177 (issue #178) and the first-investment equilibrium (issue #200): every installed firm may stop operating (shutdown boundary at ℓ = 0, default boundary with debt), debt is priced with the recovery rule, and the coupon is the par coupon or the fair coupon at issuance. `LeveredPreemptionGame(ExactPreemptionGame)` overrides the subgame (`LeveredSubgame`: `leader_value`, `follower_value`, `leader_claims(coupon)`, `leader_credit(X)`); the rent-equalization and leader-optimization routines are inherited. Claims are built from `PiecewisePowers` forcings and `SegmentClaim` (closed-form Green's function, value and slope), plus a tabulated `ForcedClaim` when the follower's scale is chosen at the switch; `installed_claims()` is the exact (coupled) equity and debt of an installed firm. `mutual_waiting_claim("reoptimized")` is the value W to each firm when neither invests before the switch, with the post-switch game priced on the levered claims (`"no_stop"` is the #178 value without the option to stop). `assigned_leader_equilibrium()` locates X_L, the maximizer of [L*(X) − W(X)] X^(−β_L⁺), and runs the no-deviation checks (the leader among triggers and, by a linear complementarity solve, among all stopping rules; the follower against its value of waiting for the leader's entry or the switch, which is not F(X); the post-switch game being a race); its docstring states the equilibrium and what is not established. Conventions are listed in the module docstring (rival never exits, zero recovery after the switch, coupon fixed at issuance). `equilibrium_record()` is one solve as a flat dict (pass `rent_equalization=` to reuse an X_P solve); `hitting_statistics()` and `expected_entry_time()` are the first-passage objects; `python -m ai_lab_investment.models.preemption_levered [fresh]` rewrites the cache.

**`robustness.py`** (issue #182): the robustness layer behind Internet Appendix D and E, at the model of record. Two sweeps over the nine calibrated parameters: ±25% one at a time (`run_sweep`, 18 draws) and joint (`run_joint_sweep`: 4,096 points of a scrambled Sobol sequence, seed 182, in the ±25% box; no unseeded randomness). At each draw `single_firm_objects` re-solves φ*, φ̲, φ̃, the post-switch policy, the precommitted-scale (X*, K*), the waiting margin of the firm that chooses its scale at the switch, and Dario's dilemma in both single-firm versions at the four matched pairs of `belief_mismatch.matched_pairs` (the draw's λ is the truth); `single_firm_signs` evaluates the qualitative statements, and `summarize_sweep` counts reversals and reports quantiles. Rows are dicts. Draws outside (A2) (`admissibility_failure`, window by parameter in `admissibility_window`) and solver failures inside it are *reported* with the reason, never dropped. The window is the one (A2) implies: the model of record does not widen it, because every pre-switch object is built on the post-switch scale problem. Duopoly objects (`duopoly_objects`: X_L/X*, X_L/X_P, K_L/K*, φ_L/φ*, X_F*/X_L, the no-deviation checks) come from the exact game at ℓ = 0 on a subsample (`duopoly_cells`: the admissible one-at-a-time draws, γ = 1.9, the first 64 joint draws admissible for the game, which also needs α < 1/2), cached in `models/robustness_duopoly.json` (`just sweep-duopoly-grid`, resumable, 100-minute cap per cell by `signal.alarm`; the cells near γ = 1.9 take about an hour each); where the game has no rent-equalization trigger on the scan, X_L is not located and the record keeps the scan-point objects (`scan_peak*`, `DUOPOLY_SCAN_FIELDS`). The joint single-firm rows are cached in `models/robustness_joint.csv` (`just sweep-joint-grid`; CSV because the JSON pre-commit hook re-indents). `policy_elasticities` generates `tbl-elasticities`; `capability_extension` the pre-positioned-capability table of Internet Appendix E (`tbl-capability`, the answer to C2: an extension built on `capability_stock.StockSpec.pre_positioned`, not the model of record). `just run-sweep` recomputes everything but the duopoly and writes four CSVs to RESULTS_DIR; the pipeline task `robustness_sweep` reads the joint cache unless `robustness.recompute_joint=true`. Paper-pinned numbers: `tests/test_robustness.py` (`TestPaperNumbers`, `TestJointSweep`, `TestDuopolySubsample`, `TestCapabilityExtension`, `TestElasticities`); update the appendix text and the test together. `ValuationAnalysis.two_period_dynamic_phi` holds capacity at the precommitted-scale K*, and `fixed_pie_robustness` reports two layers that must not be mixed: the exact primitives under the arithmetic-mean contest (`LeveredPreemptionGame(contest="arithmetic_mean")`, where the post-switch game is not a race and X_L is therefore not computed) and the nested pure-power game.

**`calibration/`** — `data.py` holds the four stylized firm archetypes (`get_stylized_firms()`: Anthropic-, OpenAI-, Google-, xAI-like) and the baseline calibration; `revealed_beliefs.py` infers implied λ from observed investment. Revealed beliefs appear in the slides and the paper's conclusion (future work), not as a paper section.

## IMPORTANT: Figure Generation

`figures/paper.py` is the single source of truth for all paper figures (11 `create_*` functions). Never add model computations elsewhere.

The other `figures/` modules produce exploratory output for the Hydra pipeline (`phase1.py`, `phase2.py`, `phase4.py`, `phase5.py`) and write to `RESULTS_DIR`. They do **not** feed the paper — do not edit them when a paper figure needs to change.

## Key Analytical Parameters

At baseline: β_L⁺ ≈ 3.01 (positive root of L-regime ODE with discount r+λ), β_H ≈ 1.55. Single firm, model of record: φ* ≈ 0.7009, X_H* ≈ 0.00278, K_H* ≈ 0.00673; precommitted-scale X* ≈ 0.00926, K* ≈ 0.01578; with scale chosen at the switch the firm waits (waiting margin 1.077; finite trigger only for λ < 0.0729). Pure-power approximation: X ≈ 0.00472, K ≈ 0.00673 (= K_H*). Duopoly, model of record (ℓ = 0, scale chosen at the switch, with the shutdown option): first investment X_L ≈ 0.00336, K_L ≈ 0.00416, φ_L ≈ 0.679 (φ* = 0.701), follower waits for the switch and enters at X_F ≈ 0.0443 with K_F ≈ 0.163; leader's boundary 0.00070 (X_L/X_D ≈ 4.8); X_L and K_L are hump-shaped in λ (peak at 0.15 on the grid). Rent-equalization trigger X_P ≈ 0.00857 (upper bound; there K_L ≈ 0.01409, φ_L ≈ 0.688, X_F ≈ 0.0859, K_F ≈ 0.317, X_P/X_D ≈ 5.9, peak at 0.10). Nested pure-power game: X_P ≈ 0.00262, K_L ≈ 0.00673, X_F ≈ 0.119, K_F ≈ 0.259. The L-regime insufficiency condition (the former A3, ratio 1.67) is descriptive only; `verify_pure_power_approximation()` in `symbolic_duopoly.py` (formerly `verify_baseline_simplification()`) reports that the pure-power form is *not* exact (A_1 ≈ −8.5e3, smooth-fit coefficient 16.5 against Γ ≈ 21.1).

## Testing

Tests are in `tests/` (one file per module; about 800 tests). `tests/test_duopoly_exact.py` covers the duopoly model of record: the claims against independent finite-difference solves, the cached equilibria against rent equalization and optimality of the leader's policy, the no-deviation conditions of the X_L equilibrium (`TestNoDeviation`), the mutual-waiting value (`TestMutualWaiting`), and the paper's duopoly numbers at X_L (`TestFirstInvestment` and the classes after it) and at X_P; tests that re-solve an equilibrium are marked `slow` and run with `just test-slow` (the default `just test` deselects them). `tests/test_duopoly.py` tests the credit closed forms and the nested pure-power game. `tests/test_base_model.py` pins the single-firm model of record (`TestSingleFirmSolution`, `TestBeliefComparativeStatics`, `TestProposition1ClosedForm`) and keeps everything about the pure-power approximation in `TestPurePowerApproximation`. Run with `just test`. `assert` statements allowed in tests. Dario's dilemma is pinned in `tests/test_valuation.py`: `TestDarioDilemma`, `TestDilemmaMatchedErrors` (additive, proportional and horizon metrics), `TestDarioDilemmaLeveraged` and `TestDuopolyDilemma` for the model of record (the last reads the cache; its two `slow` tests re-solve a belief each), and the `TestPurePower*` classes for the earlier approximation. Several tests pin paper numbers (e.g. `TestAppendixERobustness` in `test_valuation.py` checks the Internet Appendix E tables) — if a model change moves those numbers, update the paper text and the test together.

The closed-form algebra these modules implement is independently machine-checked in Lean (`../lean/`, see `lean/README.md`). Changing a closed form means checking the corresponding Lean theorem too.
