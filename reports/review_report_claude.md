#### 1.2 Proofs — **HAS ISSUES** (one substantive, two prose)

**(a) Separability of K\* and φ\* (Prop 1, Steps 1–6):** PASSES. The NPV-at-trigger algebra, h(K,φ) reduction, K-FOC cancellation via A_eff = g(φ)K^α, Inada behavior, strict concavity, and Step 6 comparative statics (w_H/w_L = λ/(r−μ_H), independent of μ_L) all check analytically and numerically.

**(b) The A₁ = 0 exactness argument (Step 5b, `_appendix.qmd:123–135`; `_model.qmd:182–188`) — the paper's weakest analytical step, with two distinct problems:**

1. *Logical non sequitur in the A₁ > 0 branch.* `_appendix.qmd:127` argues: "The option value F_L(X) ∼ A₁X^{β_L⁺} therefore eventually exceeds any investment NPV, **implying the existence of a finite L-regime exercise boundary**." That is backwards: an option value exceeding NPV everywhere implies the option is *never* exercised. The correct direction (which the A₁ < 0 branch gestures at) is a no-bubble/transversality argument, or: a positive homogeneous coefficient can only be sustained by value-matching at a finite L-regime trigger, which (A3) precludes. As written, the key step is a non sequitur — and it is precisely the sub-argument the Lean scope paragraph says is *not* formalized, so nothing protects it.
2. *The "exactness" claim is overstated.* `_model.qmd:187` states the simplified F_L is "**not an approximation but an exact consequence of Assumption (A3)**." But eq-hjb-L's forcing term λB_H X^{β_H} uses the H-regime *option* form, valid only below X_H\* — and at baseline X\* = 0.00472 > X_H\* = 0.00278, so on (X_H\*, X\*) the stated F_L does not solve the true coupled ODE. Numerically, the forced-ODE coefficient C = 21.14 exceeds the smooth-fit coefficient B = NPV(X\*)/X\*^{β_H} = 16.52, so the stated pre-trigger value exceeds the exercise payoff by ≈28% at the paper's own trigger, and the exact two-term free-boundary system (A₁ free, C fixed, VM+SP) has **no root** on (0.2X\*, 5X\*). The quantitative results actually rest on the *disclosed* "unconditional-A_eff / smooth-fit" convention (end of Step 5b; Internet Appendix B "Discounting conventions"), which is fine — but the main text should say so rather than claim exactness. **Fix:** restrict the F_L = C·X^{β_H} statement to X < X_H\*, repair the A₁ > 0 branch, and reword `_model.qmd:187` to name the convention.

**(c) Two-channel ∂X_D/∂λ (Prop 2(ii)):** PASSES. ∂A_eff/∂λ = [b(r−μ_L)−a]/Δ² verified by hand; the log-decomposition, rearrangement to the exact net threshold, regularity condition, nesting φ̃ → φ̲ as m→0, and λ-dependence of φ̃ are all correct and reproduced numerically. Trivial nit: "symmetric capacities" (`_model.qmd:295`) also requires symmetric φ for s^L = s^H.

**(d) Dario's dilemma Taylor argument (Numerical Finding 1):** conclusion verified (W‴(0.10) ≈ +3.36 > 0 by five-point finite differences; asymmetry ratio grows 1.5→6.3 with mismatch size), **but the stated mechanism is partly false**: the parenthetical at `_appendix.qmd:270` — "λ ↦ φ\*(λ) is concave (the elasticity ε < 1 and decreasing in λ)" — fails on the pessimistic side, which is the side the argument is about. Using the paper's own formula ε = (1−φ\*)/(1−α): ε(0.02) = 1.44, ε > 1 for all λ < 0.047, and φ\*(λ) is *convex* for λ ≲ 0.03. The W‴ > 0 sign survives (it is numerically established and the label "Numerical Finding" is honest), but the prose mechanism needs rewording — it is the *high* elasticity at low λ that amplifies pessimistic-side distortions.

## Part 2: Paper Review

### 3. Paper Content Review

#### 3a. Structure and Argument

**Motivation — PASSES.** The introduction is compelling: concrete capex hook, the executive-timeline dispersion, and a precisely stated gap (real options take capacity *use* as given; R&D races treat pre-breakthrough spend as pure cost; structural credit models lack the allocation-linked growth option). The training-survival channel is correctly billed as the primary contribution. Three fixes: (i) intro ¶2 is repeated nearly verbatim in `_model.qmd` (§Training-inference allocation, lines 33–36) — cut one; (ii) "the leader invests at a trigger roughly 43% below the monopoly benchmark" is a baseline-calibration value of a numerical object, not a general result — say "at the baseline calibration"; (iii) "analytical investment triggers" (abstract and intro) is mildly oversold since the trigger @eq-trigger-phi is implicit (as `_model.qmd:195` itself notes) — "closed-form capacity and allocation; implicit trigger" is accurate.

**Literature positioning — HAS ISSUES (one falsifiable claim).** Coverage of real options, strategic investment, R&D races, credit, and AI economics is good, but the claim that existing structural credit models "lack the regime-switching growth option" (`_literature.qmd` ¶4 and the marginal-contribution paragraph) is falsifiable by citation: **Hackbarth, Miao & Morellec (2006 JFE), Chen (2010 JF), and Bhamra, Kuehn & Strebulaev (2010 RFS)** build exactly Leland-type models with regime-switching cash flows and regime-dependent default boundaries. They *do* lack the capacity-allocation margin, so the contribution survives — but Proposition 2 must be positioned against this literature explicitly, especially Hackbarth–Miao–Morellec. Also worth adding: Aguerrevere (2003 RFS, 2009 JF) on capacity–competition–returns (close to testable prediction 2), and at least one OM capacity anchor (e.g., Van Mieghem's capacity review; Chod–Rudi) given the cover letter's finance–operations pitch to Management Science — an OM-adjacent referee will notice the absence. Minor: Aghion–Jones–Jones for the AI-economics paragraph. One grammar fix: `_literature.qmd:9` "the closest strategic investment paper in which they study a duopoly"; λ is also used there before being defined.

**Model building — PASSES with two substantive pushbacks.** The single-firm → duopoly progression is natural; the duopoly justification (concentration facts, N>2 footnote) is adequate. But: (1) **The leader convention is load-bearing for the headline numbers.** `_model.qmd:146–148` candidly fixes the leader's (K, φ) at the monopoly-phase optimum; X_P comes from rent equalization against an L(·) computed under that convention, so the 43% preemption discount is quantitatively conditional on it. The claim that the convention "does not affect the rent-equalization logic" is true of the logic, not the numbers — report the preemption discount under leader re-optimization (even in the Internet Appendix). (2) **The equilibrium's scale asymmetry needs confronting:** at baseline the follower installs K_F ≈ 39× the leader's capacity and enters at ~44× the leader's trigger. The mechanics are transparently explained (elasticity wedge α(2−s_F)), but as a description of AI-lab rivalry it is strained and partly an artifact of (1); the paper should address it head-on rather than leave the reader to compute the ratio. Related: the model's λ is exogenous — training is purely appropriative and does not hasten the regime switch, which sits oddly with the scaling-law motivation; state plainly (in the R&D-race comparison) that unlike Loury/Reinganum, investment does not affect the breakthrough hazard, and defend the simplification.

**Key assumptions — PASSES except A3's presentation.** Assumption 1 (A1–A4) is clearly stated with economic content (`_model.qmd:116–127`). The A₁ = 0 discussion is exemplary in *intent* (it flags exactness as the point of A3), but the substance has the problems detailed in §1.2(b): the main-text "not an approximation" sentence overstates, and the appendix branch argument is a non sequitur as written.

**Conclusion — one tension to reconcile.** `_conclusion.qmd:19` argues "the cost of overinvesting under moderate beliefs is too high for strategic posturing or agency problems alone to explain the observed behavior." This is defensible for *extreme* overinvestment (λ_invest = 0.5 loses ~23% plus 8× default risk) but sits in tension with Numerical Finding 1's headline (moderate overinvestment is cheap: 5.6% at λ_invest = 0.20) and with `_valuation.qmd:139`'s own "underinvestment is more informative than overinvestment." Either scope the conclusion's claim explicitly to extreme commitments (citing the 23%/8× numbers) or soften it.
