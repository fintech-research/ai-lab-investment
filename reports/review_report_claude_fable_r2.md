**Reviewer:** claude-fable-r2

## Executive Summary

This is a carefully executed project in which the code and the paper are unusually tightly coupled — and, on the dimensions that matter most, correct. I verified the three propositions analytically by hand against the source code (closed-form K\*, the φ\* first-order condition, the faith-based survival threshold φ̲ = R/(1+R), the characteristic roots, the Leland default boundary, and the equity/debt valuation formulas) and found exact agreement. I then re-ran the model and reproduced **every headline number in the paper to the reported precision**: X\* = 0.00472, K\* = 0.00673, φ\* = 0.7009; preemption discount X_P/X_L^mono = 0.573; credit spreads 0/12/41/97 bps and 5-year default probabilities 0.63/1.80/4.85/12.98% at ℓ = 0.05/0.20/0.40/0.70; dilemma losses 26.2%/5.6% (and 21.4% levered at λ_invest = 0.50, default probabilities 0.79% vs. 5.04%); the ~3% coupled-boundary bias (3.22%, uniform across leverage, conservative direction as claimed); the Proposition 2(ii) channel ratio ("approximately one-third": measured 0.342); and all three Appendix E robustness tables. The test suite (213 tests, all passing in ~10s) has a genuinely strong core, including a Monte Carlo verification of the first-passage formula and regression pins for the paper's headline numbers.

Two clusters of issues prevent a clean pass. First, one substantive claim in the paper is **contradicted by its own model**: the Equity Valuation Sensitivity section asserts that equity value is "locally convex" in λ over [0.1, 0.5], but the option value F(X, λ) = C(λ)·X^β_H is concave in λ over essentially that entire range (the inflection is near λ ≈ 0.08), and the model section itself correctly states that F_L "increases concavely in λ." Testable Prediction 3 in the Discussion rests on this convexity claim. Second, there is an asymmetry in verification infrastructure: the three Appendix E robustness tables are produced by functions in `valuation.py` that are called by nothing — not the pipeline, not the figure script, not the tests — so the published robustness numbers are reproducible only by ad hoc invocation (I confirmed they do reproduce, with one rounding-level discrepancy) and are unprotected against regression. A handful of disclosure gaps (the credit-risk evaluation point φ = 0.5, K = 1.0, X = 0.10 is stated nowhere in the paper; the leader-value discounting convention) and calibration soft spots (α = 0.40 sits very close to the interior-solution bound of ≈ 0.36, with inconsistent justifications) round out the list.

Overall assessment: the code is correct and the implementation faithful to the mathematics. The paper is close to submission-ready but not there yet: the convexity claim must be fixed, the Appendix E generation path must be wired into the test suite, and the disclosure gaps should be closed. My recommendation is **revise and resubmit** quality work targeted at the **Review of Financial Studies**.

## Part 1: Code Validation

### 1. Mathematical Correctness

#### Propositions vs. code — **passes**

**Proposition 1 (optimal K\*, φ\*).** I derived the FOC from the objective h(K, φ) = A_eff^β_H / b(K)^(β_H−1) by hand and confirmed the closed form K\* = [δ(αβ_H − β_H + 1) / (rc(γ(β_H − 1) − αβ_H))]^(1/(γ−1)) follows, with both numerator and denominator positive under (A2). Numerically, the closed form gives K\* = 0.006727, and the Nelder-Mead optimizer in `SingleFirmModel.optimal_trigger_capacity_phi()` (`base_model.py:497`) returns the same value; the test `test_closed_form_K_star_matches_optimizer` (`test_base_model.py:323`) enforces this. The φ\* FOC w_H/w_L = ((1−φ)/φ)^(α−1) gives φ\* = 0.7009 analytically (λ A_H = 1.667, so φ/(1−φ) = 1.667^(1/0.6) = 2.343), matching the optimizer. K\*-independence of φ is directly tested (`test_K_star_independent_of_phi`, `test_base_model.py:339`). The implementation of A_eff in `_effective_revenue_coeff_single()` (`base_model.py:431-454`) matches eq-a-eff exactly, including the φ = 0 and λ = 0 nesting limits.

**Proposition 2 (default boundary, faith-based survival).** `DuopolyModel.default_boundary()` (`duopoly.py:365-413`) implements X_D = [β⁻/(β⁻−1)]·(c_D/r + δK/r)/A_eff exactly as in eq-default-boundary, with the negative root computed at the effective discount r + λ (`_negative_root`, `duopoly.py:499-516`) — I verified the root formula and the smooth-pasting derivation by hand. The threshold φ̲: R = (0.06/0.11)^2.5 = 0.2197, φ̲ = 0.180, matching the paper. I verified the appendix's two-channel decomposition numerically: at (φ\*, K\*, ℓ = 0.40), dX_D/dλ = −0.00138 < 0, with β-channel/A_eff-channel ratio = **0.342**, matching the appendix's "approximately one-third" claim. I also confirmed dM/dλ = 1/[(β⁻−1)²D] analytically. The coupled-boundary verification (`default_boundary_coupled`, `duopoly.py:415-497`) correctly implements the C₂ = −λD_H/Q_L(β_H⁻) forcing term, and I measured the single-boundary overstatement at 3.22% for ℓ ∈ {0.2, 0.4, 0.7} — matching "approximately 3%, uniformly across leverage levels," in the conservative direction claimed.

**Proposition 3 (preemption equilibrium).** `solve_preemption_equilibrium()` (`duopoly.py:978-1060`) implements exactly the procedure described in Appendix B: 500-point grid on (X_D, X_L^mono), sign-change count (the `single_crossing` flag), Brent refinement with xtol = 1e-10. At baseline `single_crossing=True` and X_P = 0.00270. Role-invariance reproduces: φ_L = φ_F = 0.7009. Proposition 3(v) reproduces: X_P/X_D = 2.84/2.75/2.64 at λ = 0.05/0.10/0.20 (paper: "≈2.8 to ≈2.6"), and X_F/X_P = 46.1/45.5/44.8 (paper: "44–46").

Discrepancies found (all minor, see Summary): the Appendix B claim of "16 deterministic starting points (log K₀ ∈ {−6, −2, 0, 2})" is accurate for the duopoly solvers (`duopoly.py:773, 905`) but not for the single-firm solver, which uses log K₀ ∈ {−2, 0, 2} — 12 starts (`base_model.py:515`). Appendix B states "convergence tolerance is 10⁻⁸ on function values," but the code sets `fatol=1e-10`, `xatol=1e-8` (tolerance is on x, not f, at 1e-8).

#### Proofs — **passes with remarks**

- **(a) Separability of K\* and φ\* (Prop 1, Step 4).** Sound: A_eff = g(φ)·K^α makes ∂ln A_eff/∂K = α/K independent of φ. The paper is appropriately careful to note this exact separability fails in the duopoly (contest shares depend on K_i) and to label the leader's monopoly-phase policy a *solution convention* — good intellectual hygiene.
- **Step 5b (A₁ = 0 exactness).** The two-sided argument (A₁ > 0 contradicts (A3) via the dominance of X^β_L⁺ at large X; A₁ < 0 contradicts the replication lower bound F_L ≥ C·X^β_H) is correct in outline. The A₁ < 0 branch implicitly relies on C·X^β_H being the value of the pure-switching strategy, which holds because that value solves the same forced ODE with no exercise boundary and the no-bubble condition kills the homogeneous terms — the proof could state this one step more explicitly, but I find it acceptable.
- **(b) Two-channel derivative ∂X_D/∂λ (Prop 2(ii)).** Complete and honest: the proposition statement itself discloses the opposing β-channel and that net dominance is verified numerically; the appendix correctly notes the sign claim requires φ "comfortably above" φ̲ (since the A_eff-channel vanishes at the threshold while the β-channel does not). My numerical check confirms both the net sign and the channel magnitudes.
- **(c) Dario's dilemma Taylor argument.** Properly labeled a "heuristic argument" under a "Numerical Finding" heading rather than a proposition — this honesty is commendable. The W′(λ_true) = 0 optimality premise is consistent with the code's construction (policy optimal under λ_invest, value under λ_true). The three-channel decomposition (capacity channel zero by Prop 1; timing weak; allocation dominant via H-share ≈ 70% of A_eff — which I confirmed: 0.929/1.326 = 70.1%) matches the numerical results.

One labeling artifact: the appendix contains "Numerical Finding 2" but no Numerical Finding 1 anywhere in the paper — a leftover from a renumbering.

#### Two model modes — **passes**

The simple mode (`installed_value`, `optimal_trigger_and_capacity`, combined A_L = (r − μ_H + λ)/[(r − μ_H)(r − μ_L + λ)], which I verified equals 1/(r−μ_L+λ) + λA_H/(r−μ_L+λ)) and the full φ-mode are internally consistent; the zero-leverage duopoly leader-monopolist coincides with the single-firm φ-mode optimum (enforced as a regression test, `test_zero_leverage_leader_matches_single_firm`, `test_duopoly.py:245`, with a documented historical bug it guards against). The paper uses the full mode for all reported results and the simple H-regime mode only for the three illustrative figures, as documented. One presentational footnote is warranted: the H-regime illustrations (fig-option-value, fig-comparative-statics) effectively set φ = 1 (revenue X·K^α), while eq-trigger-H is written with (φK)^α; the reader is never told which φ the figures use.

#### Numerical methods — **passes with remarks**

- Multi-start Nelder-Mead with log-K transform and explicit φ-bounds rejection (returning 1e20) is appropriate for this smooth 2-D problem; Brent's method for the preemption root with a grid-confirmed bracket is correct.
- `revealed_beliefs.py`: the closed-form φ-inversion λ = (φ̂/(1−φ̂))^(1−α)(r−μ_H) (`infer_lambda_from_phi`, lines 193–221) correctly inverts the allocation FOC and is globally monotone — this is the only inversion the paper relies on, and its bands reproduce exactly. However, **`infer_lambda_from_trigger` (lines 98–134) is vacuous as shipped**: with the default `regime="H"`, the H-trigger does not depend on λ at all (verified: X\*_H identical at λ = 0.001 and λ = 2.0), so the sign-change precondition always fails and the function returns None for every input. The docstring presents it as a working inversion. The capex-intensity inversions (lines 136–187, 276–334) are non-monotone at low λ (confirmed numerically: intensity dips then rises) and return None for three of the four archetypes at the default X_ref; their docstrings concede "illustrative orderings, not point identification," and the paper prudently does not use them — but they still run in the pipeline as if informative, and the 1e10 failure sentinel inside the objective could in principle hand `brentq` a spurious sign change.
- Minor: `_follower_trigger` (`duopoly.py:677-704`) derives X_F from smooth pasting on the *linear* payoff A_eff·X − total cost, while `follower_option_value` (line 947) value-matches against the *full* equity value including the default-option term — a small internal inconsistency (the omitted term is ≈ (X_F/X_D)^β⁻ ≈ 1.5% of the default claim at high leverage). Worth a sentence in Appendix B.

#### Parameter consistency — **passes**

All nine baseline values in `parameters.py` match `_calibration.qmd`, `tbl-parameters`, and `tbl-baseline-results` exactly; the four archetype rows in `tbl-firms` match `data.py:118-177` field-for-field; derived quantities (β_L⁺ = 3.015, β_H = 1.553, A3 ratio 1.67, A2 ratio 0.890) all reproduce. Units are handled honestly: the paper is explicit that the model is unit-free under c = 1 and the $23B rescaling example checks out (0.26^1.5 ≈ 0.133 → ≈$3B).

#### Regime switching — **passes**

`simulate_demand` (`base_model.py:327-372`) implements exact-GBM steps with regime-dependent drift, switching probability λ·dt only from state L, and an absorbing H (once `regime=1`, it stays). Matches eq-gbm and the absorbing-state assumption. The characteristic roots use discount r + λ in regime L and r in regime H, exactly as in the paper's ODEs.

#### Default probability — **passes**

`default_probability` (`valuation.py:160-227`) implements P = Φ(−d₁) + (X_D/X)^(2ν/σ²)Φ(−d₂) with ν = μ_L − σ²/2, matching eq. in `_valuation.qmd:68-70` term for term, with the L-regime drift as the paper specifies. The suite verifies it against a 20,000-path Monte Carlo with Brownian-bridge correction (`test_first_passage_probability_against_monte_carlo`, `test_valuation.py:119`) — the most rigorous test in the repository.

### 2. Code Quality and Testing

#### Test coverage — **has issues**

`uv run pytest --cov`: **213 passed in 9.78s** (the review instructions say 196; the count has grown). Model-layer coverage: `base_model.py` 89%, `duopoly.py` 84%, `parameters.py` 76%, `symbolic_duopoly.py` 83%, **`valuation.py` 57%**. The `valuation.py` gap is concentrated in exactly three functions — `dario_dilemma_duopoly` (lines 591–688), `two_period_dynamic_phi` (694–824), `fixed_pie_robustness` (830–906) — which generate the three Appendix E tables (`tbl-duopoly-dilemma`, `tbl-dynamic-phi`, `tbl-fixedpie`). These functions are **called by nothing**: not the pipeline, not `generate_figures.py`, not the tests. I invoked them manually and confirmed they reproduce the published numbers (duopoly dilemma 38.3%/17.3% vs. published 38%/17%; fixed-pie preemption discount 0.628 vs. 0.63; the dynamic-φ table row-for-row — except the κ = 0 value gain, where the code yields **1.6%** against the published **1.7%**). So nothing is fabricated, but the paper's robustness appendix rests on dead code with hand-transcribed output and zero regression protection, in contrast to the main-text numbers, which are all pinned by tests (`test_baseline_loss_magnitudes`, `test_baseline_default_probs`, `test_band_matches_paper_ranges`).

#### Test meaningfulness — **passes with remarks**

The core is genuinely strong: free-boundary verification (`test_smooth_pasting`, `test_value_matching`), closed-form-vs-optimizer cross-checks, the K\*–φ separability test, Leland smooth-pasting and boundary-optimality tests (`test_smooth_pasting_at_default_boundary`, `test_duopoly.py:438`), debt value-matching and the absolute-priority cap, the MC first-passage check, and two documented regression tests for real historical bugs (leverage-coordinate drift; an r+λ discounting error in the negative root). Against this, roughly 15 tests are trivial or tautological (formula tests that re-implement the same one-line expression, e.g. `test_base_model.py:35`, `test_parameters.py:34-38`; five key-presence-only `TestSummary` classes) and several are misnamed (`test_higher_lambda_lower_trigger`, `test_calibration.py:72`, only asserts positivity).

The single most important economic gap: **the faith-based survival mechanism — a named, headline result — has no direct numerical test.** No test checks ∂X_D/∂λ < 0 for φ > φ̲, the sign reversal below φ̲, or the threshold formula itself (φ̲ appears only inside the untested Appendix E functions). I verified the mechanism manually (it holds), but the suite would not catch a regression in it.

#### Edge cases — **has issues**

Well covered: leverage = 0 (throughout), λ ≈ 0 (as 1e-10), invalid-parameter validation. Gaps: no σ → 0 or β_H → 1⁺ degeneracy tests; λ = 0 exactly never exercised against the `Q_L → 0` guard; no test near the Q_L(β_H) = 0 resonance where C blows up (guarded in code at `base_model.py:166` but untested); leverage → 1, coupon = 0, b ∈ {0,1} untested; the high-α two-term-F_L fixture tests `pytest.skip` if the fixture stops producing an interior L-trigger — the entire two-term branch verification could silently vanish. Several tests are conditionally vacuous (assertions inside `if "error" not in result:` or `if eq["X_default_follower"] > 0:` guards — e.g. `test_default_boundary_below_trigger`, `test_duopoly.py:511`).

#### Numerical stability — **passes**

Denominator guards (`denom_L <= 0`, `a_eff <= 0`), 1e20 objective sentinels, `np.clip` on probabilities and φ, an `fsolve` convergence check with logged fallback in the coupled boundary, and log-K parameterization to enforce positivity. No overflow-prone exponentials spotted. The `if sigma > 0` check in `default_probability` is vestigial (σ > 0 is validated at construction).

#### Code organization — **passes with remarks**

The parameters → base_model → duopoly → valuation hierarchy is clean and the figure-logic rule (all computation in `figures/paper.py` or models) is respected with one exception noted in §4. Smells: legacy aliases (`_phi = _option_premium_ratio`; `contest_share_L_fixed_pie` simply delegates to `contest_share_L`); the endogenous-λ machinery (`xi`, `lam_0`, `eta`, `lambda_tilde`) is plumbed through the entire duopoly but switched off (ξ = 0) and never used by the paper — the docstring at `duopoly.py:13-20` advertises a feature the paper does not contain; `pipeline.py` retains template placeholders (`simulations`, `main_regressions`, and data steps that only log); `two_period_dynamic_phi` re-implements A_eff arithmetic inline (`valuation.py:737-752`) rather than reusing the model's, the very duplication the project's own AGENTS.md forbids; `dario_dilemma_duopoly` has a dead `V_duo` branch (line 640) and embeds the X₀ = min(X)·0.5 normalization as an undocumented choice.

#### Reproducibility — **has issues**

`just run-pipeline` runs but **does nothing by default**: every task flag in `conf/config.yaml` is `false`. With flags enabled, the pipeline generates exploratory (phase) figures, not the paper figures; the paper figures come from `paper/generate_figures.py`, which is deterministic (the one stochastic figure seeds `rng(42)`, `paper.py:39`). The paper's tables are not generated by any script — `tbl-baseline-results`, `tbl-elasticities`, and the Appendix E tables are hand-transcribed into the .qmd files. Everything I tested reproduces, but the path from code to manuscript numbers is manual at exactly the points where errors creep in (and one 1.6 vs. 1.7 discrepancy did).

## Part 2: Paper Review

### 3. Paper Content Review

#### 3a. Structure and Argument

- **Motivation — passes.** The introduction is compelling and concrete (the $200B/2024 and Stargate hooks; the Pichai/Huang asymmetric-risk quotes; the Amodei "no hedge on Earth" framing). The core question — timing, sizing, and *allocating* under regime uncertainty — is articulated crisply, and the "missing interaction" paragraph (existing frameworks illuminate parts but not the training-survival link) is an effective statement of the gap.
- **Literature positioning — passes.** The review is well organized around four building blocks with the marginal contribution stated explicitly at the end. Coverage of real options (McDonald-Siegel, Dixit-Pindyck, Guo-Miao-Morellec, Décamps et al.), strategic investment (Grenadier, Huisman-Kort, Pawlina-Kort, Lambrecht-Perraudin), R&D races (Loury, Lee-Wilde, Reinganum, Harris-Vickers), structural credit (Merton, Leland, Hackbarth, Kumar et al.), and AI economics (Kaplan, Hoffmann, Agrawal et al., Korinek, Jones) is appropriate. Two possible additions: the operating-leverage/risk literature is cited only in a footnote although δ is doing real work in the K\* interior optimum, and the disagreement literature (Harrison-Kreps, Scheinkman-Xiong) is acknowledged but the distinction — comparative statics in λ rather than equilibrium under heterogeneous priors — could be made more prominent, since "Dario's dilemma" reads like a heterogeneous-beliefs result but is not one (the duopoly is solved under common beliefs, as the paper states at `_model.qmd:380`).
- **Model building — passes.** The single-firm → duopoly progression is natural; the duopoly justification (concentrated frontier-training market, minimal model with the key forces) is convincing and the N > 2 extension is appropriately deferred with a citation.
- **Key assumptions — passes.** (A1)–(A4) are clearly stated with numerical verification at baseline. The (A3) treatment is a strength of the paper: the simplified F_L = C·X^β_H is established as *exact* under (A3) rather than an approximation, with a full argument in Step 5b and an explicit boundary analysis (Appendix B notes (A3) fails for α > 0.67 and (A2) fails at three of the four archetype WACCs, with the fallback documented). One caveat: the paper does not explicitly state that the *leader's* value construction also lives inside this convention — the monopoly-phase dilution factor (X/X_F)^β_H (`duopoly.py:844`) discounts the follower-entry revenue drop with the β_H exponent rather than a first-passage factor, consistent with the unconditional-A_eff framework but never stated; a sentence in Appendix B would close this.
- **Conclusion — passes.** Summarizes without overclaiming; the "consistent with genuinely optimistic beliefs" claim is explicitly hedged as illustrative rather than identification, and the limitations paragraph is unusually candid.

#### 3b. Writing Quality

- **Clarity — passes, with one substantive error.** The prose is precise and the result taxonomy (`tbl-result-taxonomy`) — labeling each result closed-form / computational verification / numerical — is exemplary practice I rarely see and that referees will reward. The one substantive failure is the **Equity Valuation Sensitivity** section (`_valuation.qmd:147-156`): the claim that equity value is "locally convex over the economically relevant range λ ∈ [0.1, 0.5]" is false in the model. Since F(X, λ) = C(λ)·X^β_H below the trigger, curvature in λ is independent of X; I computed second differences of the φ-aware option value across λ ∈ [0.05, 0.6] and they are negative from λ ≈ 0.1 onward (inflection near 0.08) — concave, not convex. This also contradicts the paper's own `_model.qmd:202` ("F_L(X) increases concavely in λ," which is correct). Testable Prediction 3 (`_discussion.qmd:155`, asymmetric response to AI news) inherits the error. The section is also the only one with no figure or table of its own. It must be corrected (the concavity direction actually yields the opposite news-asymmetry prediction) or rebuilt around an object that is genuinely convex over the claimed range, if one exists.
- **Notation — passes with nits.** Symbols are defined before use and `tbl-notation` helps. Nits: c (cost scale), c_d (coupon rate), and c_D (coupon payment) coexist — defined carefully at `_model.qmd:263` but easy to trip over; the option-premium ratio Φ_L appears in (A3)'s discussion (`_model.qmd:188`) before the symbol is formally introduced (it is defined only inside the Step 5b proof); "Numerical Finding 2" with no Finding 1.
- **Length and focus — passes.** Appropriately scoped; no section feels padded. The Welfare subsection of the Discussion is thin (three short paragraphs of conjecture about an extension that exists in the code as the dormant ξ > 0 machinery but is never solved) — either solve a version or trim further.
- **Abstract — passes.** Conveys contribution, mechanism (faith-based survival, with the memorable "hope... worthless to creditors" line), and the two-sided dilemma result accurately and concisely.

#### 3c. Journal Fit

- **Contribution significance.** The genuinely novel content is the training-inference allocation under regime-specific contests and its coupling to the default boundary: φ\* pinned by beliefs and not market position (role-invariance), faith-based survival with a closed-form threshold, and the two-faces-of-credit-risk decomposition (lower default probability, higher loss given default — a clean, original prediction). This is a real mechanism, not a re-packaging. Against this: the building blocks are all standard (Huisman-Kort + Leland + Guo-Miao-Morellec + Tullock), the deepest strategic results (Prop 3 uniqueness, role-invariance) are computational regularities rather than theorems, and the empirical content is a stylized four-archetype calibration with no estimation or testing.
- **Methodological rigor.** Meets the technical bar: the approximations are quantified (3% boundary bias, with direction), the analytical status of every claim is tabulated, and the numerical procedures are documented and (mostly) verified. The honesty about what is proved vs. computed is above the norm.
- **Formatting and conventions.** Consistent with finance-journal conventions; the named-quote framing ("Dario's dilemma") is unusual for Econometrica but fine for JF/RFS.
- **Recommendation: RFS.** Econometrica is the wrong target: the theory, while correct, is not technically deep by their standard (the propositions are FOC manipulations and an IVT argument; the load-bearing strategic results are numerical). Between JF and RFS, the paper's strengths — a structural mechanism connecting investment, capital structure, and credit risk, with quantitative discipline — fit RFS's taste well; JF is plausible but would likely demand more empirical content for a topic this applied. (Outside the requested set, the Journal of Financial Economics would also be natural.)

## Overall Recommendation

**Revise and resubmit.** The implementation is correct and faithful to the mathematics — every proposition formula matches the code, every headline number reproduces, the approximations are quantified and conservative, and the result-status taxonomy sets a standard of transparency referees will appreciate. The required revisions are real but bounded: one substantive textual error (the convexity claim and its downstream prediction), hardening of the robustness-table generation path, a handful of disclosure additions (credit-risk evaluation point, leader-value convention, α's admissibility bound), and test-suite patches for the faith-based survival mechanism and Appendix E. None of these threatens the paper's central results.

**Recommended target: Review of Financial Studies.** The paper's core strength — a structural mechanism linking the capacity-allocation decision to default risk, with sharp cross-sectional predictions (role-invariant training fractions; lower default probability but higher loss given default for training-intensive firms) and disciplined quantification — fits RFS well. Econometrica is not the right venue: the load-bearing strategic results are computational regularities, honestly labeled as such, rather than theorems. JF is a plausible alternative, but the absence of any empirical test beyond a stylized four-firm calibration makes RFS the better-odds submission; strengthening the empirical content (even an event-study sketch of the news-asymmetry prediction, once its sign is corrected) would improve the case at either journal.
