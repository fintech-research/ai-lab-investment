## Executive Summary

The single-firm model is in excellent shape. The mathematics of Proposition 1 (closed-form K*, interior φ*, separability), Proposition 2's default boundary and faith-based survival threshold, and the Dario's dilemma machinery are correctly implemented, and I independently reproduced essentially every single-firm number in the paper: X* = 0.00472, K* = 0.00673 (matching the Proposition 1 closed form to 8 significant digits), φ* = 0.7009, φ̲ = 0.18, the value-loss asymmetry (26.2% vs. 5.6%), the leveraged default probabilities (0.79%/5.04%/0.64%), the credit-spread levels (170/259/312 bps), and the elasticity formula ε_{φ*,λ} = (1−φ*)/(1−α). The paper itself is well-motivated, clearly written, unusually candid about which results are analytical versus computational (the result-taxonomy table is a model of good practice), and the training-survival channel is a genuinely interesting mechanism.

However, I found a **critical bug in the duopoly layer** that contaminates every duopoly equilibrium number reported in the paper. In `duopoly.py`, the follower and leader optimizations run Nelder-Mead over `[log K, φ, leverage]` with the leverage coordinate free to drift up to 0.95 even when the model is all-equity. Because debt is mechanically subsidized in the objective (coupon rate 5% < discount rate 12%, debt assumed issued at face value), the optimizer always drives leverage to the 0.95 penalty bound; the returned leverage is then clipped back, but **the capacity retains the leverage-0.95 optimum**. At baseline ℓ = 0, the leader-as-monopolist solution should equal the single-firm optimum (K = 0.0067, X = 0.0047) — the objectives are mathematically identical — but the code returns K = 0.0338 and X = 0.0163, exactly the optimum under ℓ = 0.95. The paper's headline duopoly results (X_F ≈ 0.57, K_F ≈ 1.30, X_H^mono ≈ 0.0163, X_P ≈ 0.0082, the preemption discount of 0.50, the monopoly-duration ratio of 40–70, Figures 5 and 7, and the fixed-pie robustness table) are all downstream of this bug. A related design problem makes "endogenous leverage" degenerate: with subsidized debt and no default-option term in the trigger objective, optimal leverage is always a corner at an arbitrary internal cap min(1.5ℓ+0.1, 0.95), so the leverage comparative statics in Figure 5 trace out the cap (a kink is visible in the published figure at ℓ ≈ 0.57), not an economic margin. Several further significant issues affect the credit-risk quantification (risk-free rate of 0.09 in code vs. 0.04 claimed in the text; recovery exceeding the face value of debt; default probabilities computed under the H-regime drift against an L-regime boundary).

My overall assessment: the conceptual framework, the single-firm analysis, and the writing are close to submission quality, but the paper is **not ready for submission** until the duopoly solver is fixed and all duopoly-dependent quantities, figures, and discussion are regenerated, and until the credit-risk calculations are made internally consistent. None of these problems looks fatal to the qualitative story — preemption, faith-based survival, and the dilemma asymmetry are likely robust — but every duopoly magnitude in the current draft is wrong, and a careful referee at a top journal would catch the visible artifacts (the kink in Figure 5; the inconsistency between X_H* ≈ 0.0028 in Figure 2 and X_H^mono ≈ 0.0163 in the text).

## Part 1: Code Validation

### 1. Mathematical Correctness

#### Propositions vs. code

**Proposition 1 (optimal K*, φ*) — PASSES, verified independently.**
- The closed-form K* = [δ(αβ_H − β_H + 1)/(rc(γ(β_H − 1) − αβ_H))]^{1/(γ−1)} (`_model.qmd:132`) evaluates to 0.0067266 at baseline; the numerical optimizer `optimal_trigger_capacity_phi()` (`base_model.py:497–544`) returns 0.0067266 — agreement to 8 significant digits. I re-derived the FOC algebra (Appendix A Step 4) and it is correct, with both the numerator and denominator signs guaranteed by (A2).
- A_eff in `_effective_revenue_coeff_single()` (`base_model.py:431–454`) matches eq-a-eff exactly; the trigger formula at `base_model.py:539–541` matches eq-trigger-phi.
- The φ* FOC, interiority (Inada), strict concavity, and the comparative statics (i)–(ii) in Appendix A Steps 5–6 are all correct; I verified the implied closed form φ*/(1−φ*) = (λA_H)^{1/(1−α)} and the elasticity ε_{φ*,λ} = (1−φ*)/(1−α) ≈ 0.499 at baseline both analytically and numerically.
- The combined simple-mode coefficient A_L = (r − μ_H + λ)/[(r − μ_H)(r − μ_L + λ)] (`parameters.py:135–137`) is the correct φ-free aggregation of eq-a-eff.

**Proposition 2 (default boundary, faith-based survival) — PASSES with one statement/proof gap (see Proofs below).**
- `default_boundary()` (`duopoly.py:356–404`) implements X_D = [β⁻/(β⁻−1)]·(c_D/r + δK/r)/A_eff exactly as in eq-default-boundary; I re-derived the Leland smooth-pasting and it is correct.
- `_negative_root()` (`duopoly.py:406–423`) correctly uses the L-regime characteristic equation with effective discount r + λ, matching the paper.
- The threshold φ̲ = R/(1+R), R = ((r−μ_H)/(r−μ_L))^{1/α}: R = (0.06/0.11)^{2.5} = 0.2197, φ̲ = 0.180 — matches the paper's 0.18; the derivation in the proof is correct.
- Equity and debt values (`duopoly.py:429–520`) match the displayed formulas in the "Equity and debt values" subsection: the default-option bracket c_D/r + δK/r − A_eff X_D and the recovery term (1−b)·A_eff·X_D are both implemented faithfully.

**Proposition 3 (preemption equilibrium) — HAS CRITICAL ISSUES.**

1. **Leverage-drift bug (critical).** `_follower_objective_3d` (`duopoly.py:581–612`) and `_leader_objective_3d` (`duopoly.py:726–756`) reject only `lev_F < 0 or lev_F > 0.95`. The cost term is b = δK/r + (1−ℓ)I(K) + c_dℓI(K)/r, so ∂b/∂ℓ = I(c_d/r − 1) < 0 whenever c_d < r: leverage is a free subsidy in the objective, and Nelder-Mead drives it to the 0.95 bound from any start, including in the supposedly all-equity model. The result is clipped (`duopoly.py:669`, `:801`) but K is not re-optimized. Verification at baseline ℓ = 0:
   - Code: `solve_leader_monopolist()` → X = 0.0163, K = 0.0338, raw unclipped optimum [log K = −3.386, φ = 0.701, **lev = 0.950**].
   - Correct (leverage pinned to 0): X = 0.00472, K = 0.00673 — identical to `SingleFirmModel.optimal_trigger_capacity_phi()`, as it must be, since the two objectives coincide at ℓ = 0.
   - The inflation factor is exactly κ(0.95)^{−1/(γ−1)} = (0.05 + 0.95·c_d/r)^{−2} ≈ 5.03, confirming the mechanism.
   - Follower at ℓ = 0 given the *correct* leader policy: X_F ≈ 0.119, K_F ≈ 0.259 (vs. the paper's contaminated X_F ≈ 0.57, K_F ≈ 1.30).
   Everything computed through `solve_preemption_equilibrium()` is downstream: X_P, X_F, K_F, X_H^mono, default boundaries in Figure 5, the monopolist curve in Figure 7, the preemption discounts in tbl-fixedpie, and the X_F/X_P ≈ 40–70 figures quoted in the proof of Proposition 3(ii).
   **Fix:** optimize over (log K, φ) with leverage fixed at the model's ℓ (the minimal fix), or, if endogenous leverage is genuinely desired, price debt fairly (proceeds = market value D(X*), not face value) so an interior leverage optimum exists.

2. **Degenerate "endogenous" leverage (critical).** Even at ℓ > 0, the follower's and leader's chosen leverage is always the arbitrary internal cap `max_lev = min(1.5ℓ + 0.1, 0.95)` (`duopoly.py:639`, `:772`). I verified: at ℓ = 0.1/0.2/0.4/0.6 the equilibrium leverage is exactly 0.25/0.40/0.70/0.95. Consequently the x-axis of Figure 5 ("Leverage D/I") is not the leverage the firms actually carry, and the kink visible in the published X_F curve at ℓ ≈ 0.57 is the point where 1.5ℓ + 0.1 hits 0.95 — a pure artifact. The paper's interpretation of the declining X_F ("the risk-shifting effect dominates: debt holders bear downside risk", `_model.qmd:304`) is not supported by the implementation: the trigger objective contains **no default-option term at all** (`_follower_trigger`, `duopoly.py:552–579`), so the decline in X_F with ℓ is entirely the mechanical coupon subsidy c_d/r < 1, not limited liability.

3. **The "leader capacity = monopolist capacity" regularity is hard-coded, not discovered (major).** `solve_leader_monopolist()` optimizes the pure-monopolist objective (`_leader_objective_3d` uses `monopolist=True` A_eff and never references the follower); there is no alternative leader optimization against the full two-phase value L(X). The paper presents this as "a regularity attributable to the dominance of the monopoly-phase FOC … across all parameterizations tested" (`_model.qmd:146`, repeated at `:387`) — but the code never tests it; it imposes it. Worse, the claim that the leader's capacity equals "the monopolist's (Proposition 1)" is numerically false in the current code (0.0338 vs. Proposition 1's 0.0067) because of bug 1. Either optimize the leader's true objective and report the comparison, or restate this as an assumption.

4. **Asymmetric treatment of the default option in the preemption gap (major).** `_leader_value_at()` (`duopoly.py:679–724`) values the leader as perpetuity-minus-costs with `max(·, 0)` and **omits the default-option term**, while the follower side (`_follower_value` → `equity_value`) includes the Leland default put. With leverage, L(X) and F(X) are therefore valued under different conventions, biasing X_P. Additionally, the follower-entry discount factor uses (X/X_F)^{β_H} (`duopoly.py:720`); a first-passage factor in the L-regime would use β_L⁺. This is consistent with the paper's stated "unconditional A_eff framework," but the leader/follower asymmetry is not.

#### Proofs

- **Proposition 1, Steps 1–6: pass.** The separability argument (Step 4) is correct since A_eff = g(φ)K^α; the concavity and Inada arguments (Step 5) are correct; the comparative statics (Step 6) are correct, including the sharp observation that φ* is independent of μ_L.
- **Step 5b (exactness of A₁ = 0): has an error in a displayed inequality.** At `_appendix.qmd:105–107` the proof asserts that (A3) Φ_L ≡ (1 − 1/β_L⁺)/α ≥ 1 "implies (β_L⁺ − 1)/(αβ_L⁺) ≤ 1 − (1 − α) = α ≤ 1". But (β_L⁺−1)/(αβ_L⁺) *is* Φ_L, so the display asserts Φ_L ≥ 1 ⟹ Φ_L ≤ α < 1 — a contradiction (at baseline Φ_L = 1.67, α = 0.40). The displayed chain is garbled; the intended point (under A3 the standalone L-regime problem violates the interior-capacity condition, so no finite L-trigger exists) is correct, but the inequality as printed is false and must be rewritten. The surrounding sign argument ruling out A₁ > 0 (via β_L⁺ > β_H growth dominance) and A₁ < 0 (via replication of the pure switching strategy) is sound.
- **Proposition 2(ii), two-channel derivative: the statement overstates the proof.** The proposition asserts "X_D is decreasing in λ, provided the faith-based survival condition holds" (φ > φ̲). The proof (`_appendix.qmd:171–175`) correctly decomposes ∂X_D/∂λ into a *positive* β-channel and a *negative* A_eff-channel and then concedes that the net sign rests on the β-channel being "approximately 52% of the A_eff-channel at baseline" and on numerical verification across calibration ranges. So φ > φ̲ is necessary for the A_eff-channel but **not analytically sufficient** for ∂X_D/∂λ < 0. The proposition text and the taxonomy table (which classifies 2(i)–(ii) as "Closed-form / implicit function") should flag the channel-dominance step as numerical. The algebra itself (∂A_eff/∂λ = [b(r−μ_L) − a]/(r−μ_L+λ)², the φ̲ closed form, dM/dλ = 1/[(β⁻−1)²D]) is correct — I re-derived all three.
- **Proposition 2(iii)–(iv): pass** as mechanical statements; the paper is appropriately careful that (iii) is an iso-X_D locus, not an optimality claim.
- **Proposition 3(i) existence: the IVT argument is fine** given continuity, but its premises are evaluated on the buggy payoffs; re-verify after the fix. The claimed verification domain also differs from the code: Appendix B says single crossing is checked "on a grid of 500 points over (X_D, X_F*)", but `solve_preemption_equilibrium()` (`duopoly.py:869–895`) checks (0.001·X_L^mono, X_L^mono) — a much smaller interval that does not extend to X_F*. Align text and code.
- **Numerical Finding 2 (Dario's dilemma) Taylor argument: pass as a heuristic.** W′(λ_true) = 0 is a correct envelope statement given how the code evaluates policies under the true process (`valuation.py:280–338`); the three-channel discussion is internally consistent with the numerics (the capacity channel is indeed zero by Proposition 1; the φ-channel dominates). The sign W‴ > 0 is honestly labeled as numerically verified.

#### Two model modes

**Passes.** The simple mode (`installed_value`, `optimal_trigger_and_capacity`) and the full φ mode (`optimal_trigger_capacity_phi`, `installed_value_with_phi`) are each internally consistent, and the simple-mode A_L correctly aggregates the φ-free case. The paper's single-firm results use the full mode as claimed. Two caveats: (a) Figures 2 and 3 (option value, comparative statics) use the *simple* H-regime mode — appropriate for H-regime objects (where revenue is X(φK)^α and φ = 1 is implicitly optimal), but `AGENTS.md`'s blanket statement "Paper figures use the full model" is inaccurate; (b) the duopoly's "monopolist" is the full mode, which makes the calibration section's description of X_H^mono wrong (see §5).

#### Numerical methods

- Nelder-Mead with 12 deterministic starts, log-K transform, xatol 1e-8/fatol 1e-10 (`base_model.py:515–529`): sound for the 2D single-firm problem. The 3D duopoly version is where the leverage-drift bug lives; beyond the bug, letting a penalty-bounded coordinate (φ, ℓ) be handled by hard 1e20 walls inside Nelder-Mead is fragile — prefer bounded methods or reparameterization (e.g., logit(φ)).
- `brentq` on the preemption gap with xtol 1e-10 (`duopoly.py:880–889`): fine, but the fallback `X_P = X_L_mono` when `gap_high <= 0` and `X_P = X_low` when `gap_low >= 0` silently return endpoints with no warning — a failed bracket should at least be flagged in the returned dict.
- `revealed_beliefs.py`: brentq with sign-change prechecks is sound, but the module has real problems — see §5.
- First-passage formula (`valuation.py:199–216`): the barrier-hitting formula P = Φ(−d₁) + (X_D/X)^{2ν/σ²}Φ(−d₂) is the correct GBM result (I verified the reflection algebra). Two consistency problems with its *use* are described in §1 "Default probability" below.

#### Parameter consistency

**Passes.** `parameters.py` defaults (r = 0.12, μ_L = 0.01, μ_H = 0.06, σ = 0.25, λ = 0.10, α = 0.40, γ = 1.5, c = 1, δ = 0.03) match tbl-parameters and tbl-baseline-results exactly; β_L⁺ = 3.0150 and β_H = 1.5529 match the quoted 3.01/1.55; the (A2)/(A3) checks (0.89, 1.67) reproduce. `calibration/data.py` archetype numbers match tbl-firms cell-for-cell (verified independently).

#### Regime switching

**Passes.** `simulate_demand()` (`base_model.py:327–372`) uses the exact GBM log-Euler step with regime-dependent drift, switch probability λ·dt per step, and absorbing H — matching eq-gbm and the absorbing-state assumption. β_H and β_L⁺ are computed from the correct characteristic equations (`parameters.py:238–247`), with the L-regime root correctly using the effective discount r + λ.

#### Default probability

**The formula is correct; its application is inconsistent (major).**
- `credit_spread_curve()` defaults to `regime="H"` (`valuation.py:218–249`), so the published Figure 6(b) probabilities (0.17%/0.57%/6.10% at ℓ = 0.05/0.20/0.70 — which I reproduce exactly) use the H-regime drift μ_H = 0.06 for first passage **to the L-regime default boundary**. The firm in the model defaults pre-switch, in regime L, where the drift is μ_L = 0.01. Recomputing with μ_L gives 0.63%/1.80%/12.98% — 2–4× larger. Meanwhile, the Dario tail-risk numbers in `_valuation.qmd:121–122` (0.79%/5.04%) are computed with `regime="L"` (`valuation.py:399–414`), so the paper's two sets of default probabilities are mutually inconsistent in their drift convention.
- The first-passage calculation also ignores the regime switch itself (a switch to H before hitting X_D effectively removes default risk and should truncate the passage probability); given a barrier derived from a regime-switching model, a pure-GBM passage probability is an approximation that should be flagged.

Leverage = 0 is well covered. #### Numerical stability

Generally careful: division guards in contest shares (`duopoly.py:107–109`, `:122–128`), `denom_L <= 0` guards, `abs(Q_L) < 1e-15` guard in the particular solution (`base_model.py:166`), log-K parameterization preventing K ≤ 0. #### Code organization

Good. The parameters → base model → duopoly → valuation layering is clean; figure logic is properly centralized in `figures/paper.py` with `generate_figures.py` as a true thin wrapper (verified). Smells: the monkey-patching of `_effective_revenue_coeff` for the fixed-pie robustness (`valuation.py:859–867`) is fragile — make the contest specification a constructor argument; the duplicated 3D multi-start loops in `solve_follower`/`solve_leader_monopolist` should be factored; legacy no-φ duopoly methods (`contest_share`, `duopoly_revenue_pv`) linger unused by the paper path.

## Part 2: Paper Review

### 3. Paper Content Review

#### 3a. Structure and Argument

- **Motivation: passes.** The introduction is compelling and topical, the capital-commitment figures are well-sourced, and the core question (timing, sizing, *and allocating* under regime uncertainty) is crisply framed. The "training-survival channel" is articulated clearly as the marginal contribution, and the mechanically-linked growth-option/default tension is a genuinely nice framing (`_introduction.qmd:12`).
- **Literature positioning: passes, with one contradiction.** Coverage of real options (McDonald–Siegel, Dixit–Pindyck, Guo–Miao–Morellec, Décamps et al.), strategic investment (Grenadier, Huisman–Kort, Pawlina–Kort, Fudenberg–Tirole), R&D races (Loury, Reinganum, Harris–Vickers), structural credit (Leland, Hackbarth, Kumar et al.), and AI economics is thorough and well-differentiated. **However, `_literature.qmd:26` states the dilemma asymmetry backwards**: "an asymmetric *Dario's dilemma* in which the training channel amplifies the cost of *overinvestment relative to underinvestment*" — the paper's actual result (Numerical Finding 2) is that *underinvestment* is costlier in expected value. This sentence directly contradicts the abstract, Section 5, and the conclusion, and must be fixed.
- **Model building: passes.** The single-firm → duopoly progression is natural; the duopoly focus is well-justified empirically (`_introduction.qmd:30–33`) with an honest footnote on N > 2.
- **Key assumptions: largely passes.** (A1)–(A4) are clearly stated with economic content. The (A3) discussion — why F_L = C·X^{β_H} is exact rather than an approximation — is one of the more careful pieces of the paper (`_model.qmd:180–186`), though the supporting Step 5b contains the garbled inequality noted above, and two quantitative support claims ("A₁ term contributes less than 0.1% of F_L"; "less than 1% error in X_D vs. a fully coupled model on a fine grid") have **no implementing code anywhere in the repository** — I could not verify either. Either include the verification code or soften the claims.
- **Conclusion: passes.** Accurately summarizes, does not overclaim, and the careful hedging of the revealed-beliefs interpretation (`_conclusion.qmd:19, 27–29`) is appropriate. One inconsistency: the abstract says "the leader trains more … than the follower," but the computed equilibrium has φ_L = φ_F = 0.7009 to four decimals; "weakly more" or a restatement is needed (see also Proposition 3(ii), where the stated mechanism implies a strict gap the numerics do not exhibit).

#### 3b. Writing Quality

- **Clarity: high throughout.** The prose is disciplined and journal-appropriate. The risk-adjustment footnote (`_model.qmd:21`) preempts an inevitable referee question well.
- **Notation: largely consistent.** Minor items: c is overloaded (cost scale c, coupon rate c_d, coupon payment c_D — acknowledged at `_model.qmd:261` but still error-prone); β_s^− is introduced with "(with s = L)" and then used only as L; Φ_L (option premium ratio) vs. φ (training fraction) vs. φ̲ is handled but deserves a sentence; tbl-notation helps.
- **Length and focus: appropriate.** ~45 pages of main text with a substantive appendix. The discussion of static-φ bias (`_discussion.qmd:44–62`) is excellent and unusually honest.
- **Abstract: passes** — concise, states mechanism and both halves of the asymmetry. Same "leader trains more" caveat as above.

#### 3c. Journal Fit

- **Contribution significance.** The training-inference allocation under regime-specific competition, the faith-based survival condition in closed form, and the asymmetric belief-cost result constitute a real contribution to the real-options/credit-risk intersection, with a topical application executed more carefully than most "economics of AI" papers. It is, however, a model-plus-stylized-calibration paper with key duopoly results resting on numerical verification — below the bar for **Econometrica** (no new theory of general applicability, no econometrics).
- **Methodological rigor.** Strong at the single-firm level; the duopoly layer is currently undermined by the implementation issues in Part 1 and the statement/proof gaps in Propositions 2(ii) and 3.
- **Formatting and conventions.** Econometrica-style bst, numbered propositions with proofs in appendix, honest result taxonomy: appropriate for JF/RFS.
- **Recommendation: target RFS** (or JF as a second choice). The paper's center of gravity — corporate investment, capital structure, credit spreads, default risk, with an applied calibration — fits RFS's corporate theory/applied theory portfolio best. JF would likely want either sharper general theory or actual empirical tests of the cross-sectional predictions in Section 6.

### Critical Issues

## Overall Recommendation

**Major revision needed before submission.** The conceptual contribution, the single-firm analysis (which I verified end-to-end), the calibration discipline, and the writing are strong — but every duopoly magnitude in the draft is contaminated by the leverage-drift bug, the leverage comparative statics rest on a degenerate corner solution with an arbitrary cap, and the credit-risk quantification has four internal inconsistencies. These are fixable: the minimal path is (i) pin leverage in the duopoly objectives and re-solve, (ii) make leverage exogenous throughout (dropping the endogenous-leverage claims), (iii) reconcile the credit-spread benchmark, recovery cap, and default-probability drift, (iv) repair the two proof blemishes and the reversed sentence in the literature review, and (v) regenerate the notebook, the affected figures, and all duopoly numbers, adding the cross-model consistency test so this cannot recur. The qualitative results (preemption acceleration, faith-based survival, asymmetric dilemma) will very likely survive, but the quantitative story — including the headline "preemption cuts the trigger in half" — must be re-established.

**Recommended target journal: Review of Financial Studies.** The paper's blend of real options, capital structure, credit risk, and an applied calibration to a topical sector fits RFS's portfolio best. Econometrica is not a fit (key duopoly results are computational, and there is no methodological innovation of general scope); JF is plausible as a second target but would likely demand either tighter general theory or empirical tests of the Section 6 predictions.
